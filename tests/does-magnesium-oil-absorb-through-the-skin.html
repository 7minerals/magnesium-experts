Does Magnesium Oil Absorb Through the Skin?

Published October 22, 2026 · By Magnesium Experts Editorial Team

The available evidence does not show magnesium oil meaningfully raising blood magnesium levels. Human studies have been small and used unreliable measures, and the review that looked hardest at the question concluded that promoting transdermal magnesium is scientifically unsupported. Some people still use it on the skin as part of a routine, but absorption into the body is unproven.

What "absorbing" would have to mean

There are two different questions hiding inside this one. The first is whether magnesium chloride can get into the skin at all. The second is whether enough of it gets through to the bloodstream to change how much magnesium your body has. Most product claims quietly treat the first as proof of the second, and it is not.

The skin is a barrier by design. Magnesium oil sitting on the surface, or even in the upper layers, is not the same as magnesium reaching the blood or the muscle underneath.

What the research has actually looked at

The most thorough look at this question is a 2017 review in Nutrients (Gröber et al., 2017). It went through the available studies on transdermal magnesium and concluded that promoting it is scientifically unsupported, and that the authors could not yet recommend it. They did not say it was disproven. They called for larger human studies.

The studies behind that conclusion are thin. One trial that used magnesium oil sprays and foot soaks looked at hair mineral content in nine people over twelve weeks and had weak statistical power. A placebo-controlled study of a magnesium lotion in 34 healthy volunteers found no significant difference in blood magnesium. Laboratory work on skin samples has shown some passage of magnesium through skin in a dish, which is a reason to keep researching, not evidence of what happens in a person.

Why this is hard to settle

Part of the problem is measurement. Blood tests are the obvious check, but the NIH notes that serum magnesium does not accurately reflect total body magnesium (NIH Office of Dietary Supplements). Hair analysis and urine tests have their own limits. When the studies are also small and short, it becomes hard to tell a real effect from noise.

That is why the honest summary is not "it does not work" but "the available evidence does not show topical magnesium meaningfully raising blood magnesium levels."

What this means in practice

The review found oral supplementation well documented for magnesium deficiency, and transdermal use not yet proven. If low magnesium is the concern, that is a question for a doctor, not a spray.

That does not make topical magnesium pointless for everyone. Some people use it on their legs or feet as part of an evening routine and say they like it, and rubbing something into tired muscles is pleasant in its own right. That is a routine, and the evidence does not let us say it is a way of topping up magnesium. Who should be careful

Magnesium can interact with some prescription medications, and the kidneys are the main way the body clears it, so anyone with reduced kidney function should talk to a doctor before using magnesium in any form. The same applies if you are pregnant or have a heart or kidney condition. Skin comfort varies from person to person, so testing a small area first is a sensible precaution.

Frequently asked questions

Does Magnesium Oil Absorb Through the Skin?
The evidence does not show that it does. The available human studies have been small and used unreliable measures, and a 2017 review in Nutrients concluded that promoting transdermal magnesium is scientifically unsupported. That is different from proving that nothing crosses the skin, which is why larger, better-designed studies are still called for.

Is topical magnesium better than oral magnesium?
Nothing in the evidence shows that it is. The same 2017 review found that oral magnesium supplementation is well documented for magnesium deficiency, while transdermal use is not yet proven. If a doctor has confirmed low magnesium, which form to use is a decision for that doctor.

Is there a high-absorption magnesium oil?
No product has been shown to raise blood magnesium more than another, because the evidence does not show topical magnesium meaningfully raising blood magnesium levels in the first place. A claim of high absorption on a label is a marketing claim, not a research finding.

Is magnesium oil actually oil?
No. Magnesium oil is magnesium chloride dissolved in water. It is called an oil because of how it feels on the skin, not because of what it is made of.

Why is it so hard to tell whether magnesium gets through the skin?
Blood tests show only a small part of the picture. The NIH notes that serum magnesium does not accurately reflect total body magnesium, and the studies that exist have been small, short, or measured something indirect such as hair content. That makes it hard to separate a real effect from noise.

Who should talk to a doctor before using magnesium oil?
Anyone with reduced kidney function, anyone taking prescription medications, and anyone who is pregnant or has a heart or kidney condition. Magnesium can interact with some medicines, and the kidneys are the main way the body clears it.
